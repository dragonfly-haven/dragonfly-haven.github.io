Making things go well

A rough map of what we think matters. Sketch, not doctrine.

1Building ASI

Superintelligence is likely coming. The question is less whether than how, by whom, and at what pace.

2Not dying in the meantime

Misuse before alignment is solved: chiefly biorisk and security. Capable models lower the bar for catastrophe; defences have to rise faster.

3Saving the dead, and saving things

Cryonics, preservation and eventual treatment. Everyone who dies before the good future arrives is a loss we might still partly undo.

And beyond people: preserving what would otherwise be lost, such as species, ecosystems, languages, cultures, records and knowledge, so the future can still recover them.

4Alignment

Making powerful systems actually want, and do, what is good.

Inner alignment

Does the trained system really pursue the objective we trained it on, or some proxy that merely scored well?

Outer alignment

Is the objective itself the right one? Specifying what we value, without loopholes, is hard.

[Most space goes here: agendas, open problems, our angle.]

5AI welfare

If the minds we build can suffer or flourish, their interests count too.

6Broad human participation

Not every human need be a principal agent, but every human's interests should be part of what the future weighs.

One mechanism: human-aligned trusts run by agents, which hold resources and act on behalf of people who cannot or do not want to steer things themselves.

Meta: what is going on?

Beneath all of the above lie questions we cannot route around:

  • The hard problem of consciousness
  • Reality and the simulation question
  • The gap between objective and subjective
  • Decision theory, anthropics and trust
  • Meta-philosophy: how to reason well about all of this at all
